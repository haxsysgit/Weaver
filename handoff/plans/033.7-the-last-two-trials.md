# Draft: Plan 033.7, The last two trials

Status: admitted 2026-11-07. The canonical plan is
`plans/033.7-the-last-two-trials.md`; this file is the descriptive draft it
was built from, kept for context.
Diagnosed 2026-11-07 from the 033.6 transcripts and the code; the owner held
it that morning, then admitted it trimmed the same day.
Depends on: Plan 033.6 (closed, accepted 2026-11-06).

## Goal

Make Weaver pass all twelve Reader Experience Trials on one build, so the
work from Plans 033 to 033.6 can go to `main`. Two trials still fail on the
evaluator record (10 of 12). Both are about Weaver following through on what
is already in front of it: its own earlier answers, and the fight the reader
actually named.

## Why it matters

The final product gate is a reader who enjoys talking with Weaver. A buddy
who forgets the list it gave you three messages ago, or who answers about a
different fight than the one you asked about, is not a buddy. These two are
also the failures most likely to show up in real use: long chats and "explain
this fight to me" are the core of the product.

## What we want to achieve

### 1. Weaver remembers its own answers (Trial 9, the long thread)

What happened in 033.6 (private transcript `033.6-long_thread`): the reader
asked for a ranking (a top ten). Weaver gave one. On a later turn, the reader
referred back to it. Weaver said "that was the whole list" as though it had
given fewer entries, then adopted a character at third place who was never on
its list (Mordret, where the list had Anvil third). Two turns later it showed
the changed order under "same three, no changes". The reasoning in each turn
was cited and fair; only Weaver's memory of itself was wrong.

What we want:

- When the reader points at something Weaver said earlier ("your list", "you
  said", "go back to your third pick"), Weaver reproduces it exactly: the
  same entries, the same order, the same claims. Its own published answers
  are as reliable to it as the chapters.
- When Weaver changes a position, it says so plainly and says why ("I'm
  moving Anvil down to fourth, because..."). It never presents a changed
  answer as unchanged, and never claims "no changes" over a change.
- When the reader pushes it to change ("put Mordret third"), Weaver either
  agrees and says what moved, or holds its ground with reasons. Either is
  fine; silent drift is not.
- Callbacks and a running joke survive eight to ten turns. Weaver does not
  repeat the same point, anecdote or phrasing across turns.
- This holds for any earlier answer, not just rankings: a verdict, a list of
  reasons, a chosen scene, a fanfic's established details.

**Cause, confirmed 2026-11-07** (observation from the 033.6 transcript and
`agent/turn.py`, not a guess). The turn brief is not the main problem and
neither is a history window: there is no window. When a skill runs a final
reread, that call is built from the current reader message alone
(`final_history` in `agent/turn.py`, around line 1067). Every earlier turn is
dropped from it. The previous answer survives only as `prior_weaver_position`
in the turn brief, cut to 2,000 characters by `_compact_position`.

So the pass that publishes cannot see a list given two turns ago. Turn 0
published a top ten with Anvil third. By turn 2 the model had only turn 1's
"Nephis over Sunny" argument in front of it, said "that was the whole list",
then adopted Mordret at third from ch. 3147. Turn 5 then showed that changed
order under "same three, no changes". The draft pass had the full history.
The reread, which is the call that ships, did not. Turn 0's answer was 2,114
characters, so the 2,000 character cut took very little: the list was lost
because nothing of that turn was in the call at all.

What to try (not approved yet): give the publication pass the thread's own
published answers in order, bounded; and check a published ranking against
an earlier published one, so a change must be announced and "no changes"
must be true.

### 2. Weaver finishes the fight that was asked (Trial 12, the confusing fight)

What happened in 033.6 (private transcript `033.6-confusing_fight`): the
reader asked Weaver to explain a confusing late-novel fight and help them
picture it. Weaver correctly pushed back that the reader had merged two
fights, and even pointed at the right one (Sunny against the Flesh of
Kanakht, after ch. 2708), but then explained Jet's duel instead. It said the
Cursed Wanderer "kept his life" when Jet killed him (ch. 2706), which it had
not read. The fight the reader meant (about chs. 2709 to 2716, ending in an
escape and the Titan sinking) was never told. Its
lookups ran out (8 of 8) at ch. 2708. On turn 4 it said "this turn only gave
me 2698", which is machinery talk.

What we want:

- Weaver identifies the fight the reader means, says which one it is (with
  chapters), and tells that one. If the reader conflated two fights, Weaver
  says so kindly and then explains the one asked for, optionally contrasting
  the other in a line.
- It reads the whole fight, from the setup to the resolution and the
  aftermath. A fight spanning ten chapters is normal in this novel; the
  reading budget must be able to reach the end of one. Weaver never narrates
  a fight from its first half alone.
- It never states an outcome (who won, who lived, who died, what was lost)
  that it has not read in this turn or a cited earlier turn. If it has not
  reached the resolution, it keeps reading or says plainly that it has not
  got there yet.
- The retelling makes the reader see it: sequence, positions, stakes,
  turning points, each fighter's motive, and the cost. It is a retelling,
  not a dry summary and not an essay. Quotes are real and short.
- "Prove it" and "huh?" follow-ups get the specific chapter and moment, not a
  rerun of the whole answer.

**Findings, confirmed 2026-11-07** (observation from the 033.6 transcript and
the skills code). Four things, in order of weight:

1. **The turn never went back to the fight the reader meant.** Turn 0 flagged
   it correctly ("the actual sunny vs kanakht scrap is the one starting there,
   against the flesh, after these pages", ch. 2708) and then spent all eight
   lookups on Jet's duel. No later turn returned to the fight it had just
   named. The reader got an explanation of the neighbouring fight and never
   the one they asked about.
2. **Eight lookups cannot reach the end of a ten chapter fight.** Turn 0 used
   8 of 8 and stopped at ch. 2708, inside the fight that runs to about 2716.
   `scene_explanation` carries `tool_budget=8` in `skills/catalog.py`.
3. **The follow-up lost the scene skill.** "who actually won?" routes to
   `quick_recall` (4 lookups) because it matches none of the follow-up
   markers in `skills/router.py`, so turns 1 to 5 had 4 lookups and no
   scene-reading plan where turn 0 had 8. That is the same class of bug 033.6
   fixed for "one more", one marker list further along. The carry machinery
   already exists (`_earlier_skill`); the message simply never reaches it.
4. **An answer contradicted its own evidence.** Turn 1 said the Cursed
   Wanderer "kept his life", when Jet killed him (ch. 2706, checked by the
   033.6 review), and the same answer quoted Sunny's line that says so. No
   check compares a stated outcome with the evidence in its own answer.

What to try (not approved yet): read the named fight to its end before
explaining an adjacent one; a scene budget that scales with the span the
chapters give (the `arc_summary` skill already runs at 20); end-of-scene
follow-up markers ("who won", "how did it end") that carry the scene skill
the way "one more" now carries discovery; and a check that an outcome cannot
contradict the answer's own quote or an opened chapter.

### 3. Weaver never describes its own machinery

Phrases such as "this turn only gave me...", "the passages I opened", "my
reading packet", "my lookups ran out" are machinery. The reader should hear
"I haven't got to the end of that fight yet, let me keep going" or simply get
the answer. The existing exposed-machinery check should catch this family of
phrases, with negative tests drawn from real transcripts so it does not fire
on normal speech ("in this chapter...").

### 4. Last-resort trims are visible in the records

Two passing trials (half-memory turn 1, theory turn 1) showed trim marks: a
cut block quote and list items reduced to fragments, most likely from the
last-resort fallback variants. We want each run to record which variant was
published (none, quotes dropped, unsupported sentences dropped, recalls
dropped), so trims can be counted and the worst one fixed on evidence. A
published answer should never read as visibly cut.

### 5. Live runs are not disturbed by sleep

The live run holds the PC awake from start to end, and records the build
hash, start and end time, and spend.

## What success looks like

- **All twelve Reader Experience Trials pass on the evaluator record in one
  run on one build**, judged on both axes, with every quote checked against
  its cited chapter. The ten that passed in 033.6 still pass; none regresses.
- Trial 9: across eight to ten turns, every reference to an earlier answer is
  exact; every change is announced; the running joke and callbacks hold.
- Trial 12: the fight told is the fight asked; the sequence and the outcome
  match the chapters; nothing is stated past what was read; the reader can
  picture it; the "prove it" follow-up lands on a specific moment.
- No machinery phrases in any published answer across the twelve.
- No gap answers, no answer rescued from a failed publication, no waking
  call over the 120 s limit.
- The check set does not get worse (4 of 9 correct or better, another scene
  presented 0 of 9). Reaching its own 6 of 9 gate is welcome but not required
  here.
- Deterministic tests cover each new behaviour, written red first. Floors
  hold or rise: backend at least 934 passed, frontend 185, `tsc` and build
  pass, ruff adds no finding on changed files.
- A fresh reviewer (a separate agent with no part in the build) reviews the
  diff before the live run; findings are repaired or recorded.
- Spend within a cap of about $1.50 (one full trial run about $0.45, one
  check set about $0.12, room for one re-run if the owner approves it).
- **The owner accepts.** Then, and only then, the owner decides whether
  `plan-030` merges to `main`.

## Constraints

- Diagnose from the private transcripts and run records first. Name the
  cause of each failure in `results.md` before writing a fix.
- The trial prompts are examples. Nothing in the fix may key on the trial

  every run, including failures.
