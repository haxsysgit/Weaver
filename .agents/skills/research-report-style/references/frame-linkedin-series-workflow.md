# FRAME LinkedIn series workflow notes

Use when drafting FRAME/Haxaml social posts for Arinze.

## Series progression

Do not compress the whole FRAME story into one post. The series should build buy-in before proof:

1. Relatable pain: coding agents are good, but project context/governance is weak.
2. Simple FRAME concept: typed context architecture / project representation.
3. Five parts: Facts, Rules, Map, Expect, Acts as five jobs, not five files.
4. Frame Lab: why FRAME needs tests instead of theory.
5. Fixtures: why different projects create different pressure.
6. Current test progress: scores/findings only after the reader understands the problem.
7. Weight/pressure method: how repeated pressure decides whether entries/blocks adjust.
8. Haxaml: runtime/tooling layer that uses FRAME.

## Post 1 role

Post 1 is the doorway, not the lab report.

It should:
- sound like live research/findings, not a finished product announcement
- avoid “I built X” framing
- introduce why FRAME started
- make the reader feel the context problem first
- mention the five reasons lightly and clearly
- avoid fixture names, scores, weight algorithms, or schema mechanics

## Voice and structure

Preserve Arinze’s rough social-media voice:
- raw but readable
- simple words
- light looseness is okay
- phrases like “basically i mean...” can help
- do not overcorrect grammar until it sounds corporate or AI-written
- remove generic AI-assistant phrases like “Honestly, I think...”
- no forced emoji; remove it if it feels pasted on

Avoid LinkedIn newline-poetry. Use real paragraphs, but section the reasons enough that the post scans.

Good structure for Post 1:
- short opening: “not another i built this post”
- simple FRAME definition
- why agents being good changes the problem
- real projects are messy
- “There are five main reasons...”
- Reason 1..5 labels, lightly expanded
- concise close tying agents to project self-representation
- hashtags: #FRAME #agent

## LinkedIn length pitfall

LinkedIn may reject long posts depending on composer/account/surface. Keep the paste-ready body safely under ~3000 characters when the user is posting immediately.

Recommended workflow:
1. Keep the long draft as reference.
2. Create a `*-short.md` posting version.
3. Measure body chars excluding metadata/frontmatter.
4. Aim for <3000 chars, ideally with some margin.
5. Preserve the five-reason structure when shortening.

Do not delete the long version unless asked; it can seed later posts.
