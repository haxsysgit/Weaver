# Assessment Answer Patterns (Arinze Voice)

Patterns that worked for AfterQuery-style assessments. These platforms test critical thinking, source evaluation, and writing quality. They also scan for AI-generated answers, so voice matters.

## The Baseline Rule

Never write answers that sound like a textbook, a chatbot, or a Wikipedia article. If your answer could have been written by anyone, it fails. Every answer should have at least one of: a personal opinion, a specific detail only you would include, an informal connector (Nah, Look, Honestly, So here's the thing), or a real example.

## Pattern By Question Type

### Fact-check / True-False
- Open with a casual verdict: "Nah, this one's completely false" not "This claim is inaccurate"
- Give the correction, not just the negation
- Explain WHY it matters, not just WHAT is wrong
- Source URL at the end, not woven into a sentence

### Source Ranking
- Lead with your ranking (A > B > C > D)
- Top choice: explain what makes it credible (methodology, peer review, institution)
- Bottom choice: explain what makes it weak (anecdotal, no controls, selection bias)
- Be opinionated. "It's entertainment, not evidence" is better than "This source lacks methodological rigor"

### Error Detection in AI Response
- State how many errors you found upfront
- For each: what it said vs what it should say
- One source per correction
- No hedging. "There's one factual error" not "It appears there may be an error"

### AI Response Comparison
- Pick a side immediately. "Response A, easily" not "While both have merits..."
- Name specific details that make the good one good (numbers, technologies, concrete claims)
- Name specific failures that make the bad one bad (vagueness, templates, buzzwords)
- Imagine a recruiter skimming fifty of these. Which one do they remember?

### Personal Essay (Invention, etc.)
- Pick something real that you actually care about
- Lead with the choice. "The internet. No question."
- Include a personal detail or two
- End with a light callback or self-aware note
- School-essay tone is death. Write like you're telling a friend.

### Constraint Evaluation
- List constraints as complete sentences, not bullet fragments
- Evaluation: one sentence per constraint. Satisfied / Failed / Mostly satisfied.
- Catch the sneaky misses (like "picking it out" instead of "hand-knit")
- Call out FAILED clearly, don't soften it

### Prompt Writing (Designing a Test Prompt)
- Pick a domain you actually know something about
- Avoid the top-3 most generic domains (bicycle repair, cooking pasta, how to write an email)
- Niche but well-known is the sweet spot (whetstone sharpening, cast iron restoration)
- Constraints should be woven into flowing paragraphs, not a labeled "Constraints:" section
- No numbered constraint lists in the prompt body. That's the #1 AI-generator tell.
- The tone should feel like you're briefing someone, not writing a spec

## Quick Voice Checklist for Assessment Answers

1. Would I say this out loud to a friend? If no, rewrite.
2. Is there at least one opinion or personal take? If no, add one.
3. Are there any AI-tell phrases (Certainly, Great question, I hope this helps)? Cut all.
4. Are the sentences varied in length? Some short. Some longer. Not all 15 words.
5. Does each answer have at least one concrete detail (a number, a name, a specific source)?
